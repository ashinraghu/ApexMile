About

ApexMile is a progressive web app. Add it to your Home Screen for full-screen use. Group riding uses a public demo broker — rooms are unauthenticated, so only share a room code with people you trust.

Privacy Riding safety build
Privacy, plainly

On your device. Your name, phone (if you add it), vehicles, fill-ups, odometer and photos are stored in this browser’s local storage. They are not uploaded to us — there is no “us” server.

When you join a group ride. While a ride room is open, your display name, status (riding normal, fuel stop, SOS), live position and course are published to a public MQTT broker so other riders in the room can see you. Room codes are short and the broker is unauthenticated: anyone who knows or guesses the code can subscribe. Phone browsers pause location updates when the app is backgrounded or the screen is off — other riders will see a “not updating” state until you return.

What we deliberately do not send. We do not send your phone number, your saved vehicles, your fill-up history or your photos in ride telemetry.

How to stop sharing. End the ride, or close the tab. Share only while the ride is live; ending a ride removes you from the room and publishes a leave message.

Maps and routing. Vector map tiles come from OpenFreeMap, satellite imagery from Esri, routing from OSRM, search from Nominatim, and toll detection from Overpass. Those requests include the coordinates or search terms you type. If you enable Live Traffic, your own TomTom key is kept in this browser’s storage and sent only to TomTom with the traffic tile requests.

Deleting your data. Garage → sign out clears the account but keeps logs. To erase everything, clear site data for this app in your browser settings.

Ride safe

Set the route before you ride. Pull over to type, change stops or invite riders. The map dims controls above walking pace for a reason.

Mount your phone. Use a bar mount and keep it out of direct sun. A glove-friendly tap is better than a squeeze at speed.

The radar is an aid, not a rescue service. Pack-gap alerts use GPS and can be wrong under tunnels, flyovers or dead zones. Do not rely on the app alone to keep a group together.

SOS is a status flag. Marking a breakdown warns riders in your room. It does not contact emergency services. In an emergency, call 112.

Battery and data. Live riding uses GPS and mobile data continuously. Carry a charger on long rides; save offline maps by keeping the app open on the radar screen before you go off-network.
